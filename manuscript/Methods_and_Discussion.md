## Methods

### Study design and sample provenance

We reanalysed accession GSE256038, a published RNA-seq experiment in rat fetal neural stem cell-derived astrocyte cultures. After seven days of exposure, four libraries were available in each of eight groups. The 50 nm and 500 nm polystyrene series each included a control and three nominal mass concentrations (1, 10 and 100 µg/mL). We matched all 32 GEO samples to their BioSample records and SRA runs. Each submitted sample title ended in a number from 01 to 04, present once per group and concordant with the within-group rank of the GEO accession. We used this number as an inferred block. Its experimental origin was unknown. The original study reported four samples for RNA extraction and three independent experiments for the separate viability assay. [Source data](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/geo/query/acc.cgi?acc=GSE256038) · [Original study](https://doi.org/10.3390/nano14050429)

### Quantification and gene-level models

We verified the 64 paired-end FASTQ files against the ENA manifest using MD5 and assessed read quality with FastQC, fastp and MultiQC. Salmon 2.8.0 quantified transcripts against rat GRCr8 cDNA and ncRNA sequences from Ensembl release 116. The transcriptome index used 31-mers without genomic decoys. Quantification used automatic library-type detection and sequence- and GC-bias correction. We aggregated transcript estimates with tximport 1.40.0 and used gene counts and sample-specific transcript-length offsets in DESeq2 1.52.0. Genes with at least ten counts in at least four libraries were retained (16,726 genes).

The full negative-binomial model included the eight-level exposure group and the inferred block. We also fitted a group-only model. For high-dose comparisons with each series’ own control, we used a Wald test, Benjamini–Hochberg (BH) adjusted P < 0.05 and an absolute apeglm-shrunken log2 fold change of at least log2(1.5). For comparison with the published gene lists, we matched Ensembl identifiers and then unique gene symbols under the repository’s matching rules. Recovery was calculated among matched published discoveries. As a negative control for blocking, we independently permuted the four block labels within each of the eight groups, refitted DESeq2 for 39 draws, and compared counts of high-dose discoveries at adjusted P < 0.05 with those under the observed labels.

### Direct comparison of exposure responses

At each dose, the contrast was the signed log2 change in the 50 nm series relative to its own control minus the corresponding change in the 500 nm series. Each contrast used unshrunken DESeq2 Wald statistics with BH adjustment across genes with evaluable adjusted P values. A positive contrast can arise from greater induction in the 50 nm series or greater repression in the 500 nm series. We also tested the three interaction coefficients jointly by comparing a full block + series × categorical dose model with a reduced block + series + categorical dose model (three-degree-of-freedom likelihood-ratio test).

For sensitivity analyses, we omitted each inferred block in turn, retaining 24 libraries per refit and the original filtered gene universe. Each refit used tximport inputs and re-estimated normalization and dispersions. Descriptive within-block differences used log2(normalized count + 0.5). The stringent high-dose set required a significant full-model contrast, concordant direction in all four observed blocks and all four omission fits, and adjusted P < 0.05 in each omission fit. Individual gene intervals are pointwise 95% Wald intervals. We also counted discoveries at full-model baseMean thresholds of 10, 20 and 50 after genome-wide adjustment.

### Targeted gene-set analyses

Following the exploratory high-dose analysis, we selected the ciliary/ependymal marker set and the complement activation set (GO:0006956) for dose-specific sensitivity analysis. For each fit and dose, genes with finite Wald statistics and an annotated symbol were ranked by the signed statistic. For duplicated symbols, we retained the gene with the largest absolute statistic, using the gene identifier to break ties. We performed preranked GSEA with clusterProfiler, using a minimum set size of four and a maximum of 500. Seven ciliary genes and 18 complement genes contributed at high dose. BH adjustment covered the two sets at three doses within each fit. For the separate high-dose gene-omission analysis, we repeated enrichment after dropping each represented member from its own set and adjusted across all 25 omissions. Descriptive block scores in Figure 1 are unweighted means of within-block differences across represented genes. The targeted tests and overlapping omission fits assess sensitivity in the discovery dataset.

## Discussion

The original study reported a broad inflammatory transcriptional response to repeated polystyrene exposure and more high-dose discoveries in the 500 nm series. Our analysis asked which changes differed directly between the two series at equal nominal mass concentration. The distinction matters because separate comparisons to control can yield different numbers of significant genes even when the underlying exposure responses are similar. The direct contrast identified a greater relative decline in ciliary/ependymal marker expression in the 500 nm series and greater enrichment of complement-related transcripts toward the 50 nm response. Both high-dose gene-set differences persisted across all six fits, although the number of significant gene-level differences was sensitive to the block term. [Original study](https://doi.org/10.3390/nano14050429)

The ciliary set also showed a difference at 10 µg/mL. Its direction persisted in the group-only model and all four block omissions, but omitting block 3 increased the adjusted P value to 0.161. This result motivates a dose-specific hypothesis. With three nonzero doses and four samples per group, the data provide limited resolution for comparing onset concentrations. The observed block scores also varied at high dose, including one ciliary score close to zero. Gene-set enrichment summarizes how member genes are ranked across the transcriptome, whereas each block score describes the average observed change within a single block. Reporting both exposes differences that an enrichment statistic alone can obscure.

Several explanations for the ciliary signature remain compatible with these data. Bulk cultures can show lower ciliary marker expression if the frequency of marker-positive cells falls, if expression falls within those cells, or if both occur. The separate MTT assay in the source study showed approximately 70% of control signal for both series at 100 µg/mL after seven days (estimated visually from Figure 2B). The assay cannot assign a viability measurement to an individual sequenced sample. Similarly, enrichment of complement transcripts does not establish complement activation at the protein level. At equal nominal mass, particle number and total geometric surface area also vary with diameter. The measured contrast therefore describes these exposure conditions and cannot isolate a diameter-specific mechanism. [Source study, Figure 2B](https://www.mdpi.com/2079-4991/14/5/429)

The suffix-based block captured reproducible structure within the dataset, but the biological or technical unit corresponding to that suffix remains unresolved. The sharp difference in gene discovery counts between blocked and group-only fits limits interpretation of the complete discovery list. Selection of the two signatures following inspection of the high-dose data also makes the targeted pathway results exploratory, even where gene and block omissions agree. The most defensible conclusion is that two transcriptional programmes distinguish the high-dose series across plausible model specifications in this study. Independent data with documented sample relationships would establish how far this finding generalizes.

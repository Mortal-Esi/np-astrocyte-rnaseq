Dear Editors,

I submit “Complement gene expression in rat astrocyte cultures exposed to 50 or 500 nm polystyrene particles at equal nominal mass concentrations” for consideration as a Research Article in BMC Genomic Data.

Marcellus and colleagues found that polystyrene exposure altered inflammatory gene expression in rat astrocyte cultures. Their public dataset, GSE256038, also permits a comparison that the original exposure-versus-control lists could not answer: at the same mass concentration, which genes respond differently to the two preparations?

I compared the change from each preparation’s own control across 32 libraries. At 100 µg/mL, the mean response of 18 complement genes was positive for 50 nm particles and negative for 500 nm particles. The difference exceeded that of genes matched on their 500 nm response by 0.457 log₂ units. Ciliary genes showed a larger raw difference, yet their matched genes changed by almost exactly as much. This distinction is the paper’s main contribution. The three follow-up sets were selected after inspection of the data, and the manuscript reports how the results depend on the inferred sample blocks.

The code, processed inputs and gene-level results are available for readers to examine and reuse. The analysis and a manuscript version are publicly archived in Zenodo (v1.3.1, https://doi.org/10.5281/zenodo.24390580). The v1.3.0 archive (https://doi.org/10.5281/zenodo.24361377) and its predecessor (https://doi.org/10.5281/zenodo.24197910) include earlier manuscript versions. The current text is available on GitHub (https://github.com/Mortal-Esi/np-astrocyte-rnaseq).

I am the sole author, funded the work personally and declare no competing interests. The manuscript has not appeared in a journal and is not under consideration elsewhere. The Methods describe the computational assistance used in the analysis and writing.
